# R001 · Bijli ka taapmaan suraj ki surface se 5 guna zyada! (Science Wala Sach)

Researched 24 Nov 2026 by web search (results read, not memory). Framing: compare with the Sun's VISIBLE SURFACE, not its core.

| # | Claim (as narrated) | Source | Verified |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Lightning can heat the air around it to about 50,000 °F (≈ 27,700 °C) | NOAA National Weather Service, "Lightning temperature" https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-temperature ; NOAA NSSL Severe Weather 101 https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/lightning/ | yes |
| C2 | That is about 5 times hotter than the surface of the Sun | Same NWS page ("5 times hotter than the surface of the sun") | yes |
| C3 | The Sun's visible surface is about 5,500 °C (≈ 5,800 K) | UCAR Center for Science Education, "The Surface of the Sun" https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/sun-space-weather/sun-photosphere ; Britannica "How Hot Is the Sun" https://britannica.com/science/How-Hot-Is-the-Sun | yes |
| C4 | It happens in a fraction of a second | NWS "Sound of Thunder" https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/thunder.html ("heated ... in a fraction of a second") | yes |
| C5 | The heated air expands explosively, then cools and contracts; that creates the sound wave we hear as thunder | NWS "Lightning science: thunder" https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder | yes |
| C6 | The Sun's core is about 15 million °C, so the Sun still wins overall (honest twist) | NASA Science "Sun facts" https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts | yes |

Arithmetic: 50,000 °F = (50,000 − 32) × 5/9 ≈ 27,760 °C → "लगभग 28 हज़ार डिग्री सेल्सियस". 27,760 / 5,500 ≈ 5.
Avoid: "lightning is hotter than the Sun" without "surface"; exact bolt width/duration (sources weak/indirect).
